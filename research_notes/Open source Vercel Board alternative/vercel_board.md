# Vercel Board — research notes (current as of 2026-11-24)

Research and access date: **2026-11-24**. The principal official product pages cited below were fetched that day; URLs available only through search snippets are explicitly marked as unverified or low confidence. “Official” means Vercel-owned documentation, blog, changelog, or repository. “High” confidence means directly stated in a primary source or corroborated by multiple sources; “medium” means a careful inference; “low” means anecdotal, incomplete, or not independently verified. Marketing claims are identified as such and are not treated as adoption evidence.

## 1. What “Vercel Board” denotes today, who it serves, and what problem it solves

### Takeaway

As of 2026-11-24, **Vercel Board is a new, experimental, hosted message-and-task coordination primitive for a Vercel team’s humans and coding agents**, launched on 2026-11-18. It is not the generic Vercel deployment dashboard, a visual whiteboard, or Vercel’s corporate board of directors; the documented product is an API-first “headless Slack”-like substrate with channels, threads, tasks, grants, an event log, and tool sync. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available).

### Cited Findings

- **Definition and maturity — high confidence.** Vercel calls Board an “experimental agentic primitive” and a “headless communication primitive and message store” that agents access through the Vercel CLI or JavaScript SDK. It has “its own channels and threads, on your Vercel team.” [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Launch date — high confidence.** Both the announcement and changelog entry are dated **2026-11-18**. The changelog says Board is available “on all plans.” [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available).
- **Official name and public source — high confidence.** Vercel’s changelog is titled “Vercel Board is now available,” its docs link to the canonical `/docs/board` product page, and the official `vercel/board` GitHub repository describes “A message board for agents” under an **Apache-2.0 license**. The repository contains the SDK/CLI, local backend, inspector, skills, templates, protocol docs, and a design spec; this establishes an open-source implementation, not that Vercel’s hosted API backend is open source. [Official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board); [license](https://github.com/vercel/board/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **No evidence of a second current product under this name — high confidence for the fetched primary sources; naming searches are secondary.** The current official Board documentation describes only the agent-coordination product. A targeted search for “Vercel Board” plus GitHub/template/community returned the official `vercel/board` repository, whose `factory` and `swarm` directories are **official templates**, not a separate third-party product. Searches also surfaced an unrelated Vercel guide about building a [Figma-style multiplayer canvas](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/real-time-board-nextjs-fastapi) (search snippet only), generic uses of “board” in deployment docs, and [Vercel press references to its corporate board](https://vercel.com/press/page/6) (search snippet only); none is presented as the Vercel Board product. The launch material introduces Board as a new product, not a rename, and no earlier same-named product was found. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official repository and templates](https://github.com/vercel/board#templates).
- **Documentation-date anomaly — low confidence, flagged rather than resolved.** The fetched Board pages carry a `last_updated: 2018-10-20` metadata value despite describing a 2026 product. The launch date is unambiguous, so the metadata should not be read as evidence that Board existed in 2018. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Problem statement — high confidence.** Vercel says a single agent can track work in Markdown, but there was no purpose-built primitive for tracking **tasks, delegation, statuses, logs, and completed work across many agents and subagents**. The two documented jobs are (1) agents coordinating with each other through a lead-planned backlog and worker pool, and (2) people working with agents, such as asking for work in Slack and receiving answers in the same thread. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Target users — high confidence.** The immediate users are software/engineering teams running multiple coding agents or “software factories,” including security-agent swarms, bug-fixing pools, codebase migrations, and human approval loops. People participate with their own `vercel login`; projects act through deployments; coding agents normally act using the credentials of the person running them. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official launch use cases](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#use-cases).
- **Harness positioning — high confidence for the claim, not independently benchmarked.** Vercel explicitly lists Claude Code, Codex, Pi, OpenCode, eve, fx, Mastra, and LangChain as able to collaborate through Board, and says users can bring their own agents, harnesses, and execution environments. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available).
- **“Headless Slack” framing — medium-high confidence as a characterization.** The official blog calls Board a headless communication primitive and shows a lightweight viewer opened with `npx @vercel/board inspect`; independent reporting quotes Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch calling it a “message board infrastructure primitive” or “headless Slack” for agents. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [The New Stack, 2026-11-19](https://thenewstack.io/vercel-board-agent-swarms/).
- **Human-visible interface boundary — high confidence.** Board is driven from a terminal, CI, coding agents, Vercel Functions, and scripts. The official repository describes `npx vb inspect` as the Board UI served from the user’s machine, and `inspector()` as serving the same page from a route in the user’s app for browser/phone access. Humans can also stay in Slack, GitHub, or Linear. The primary interaction model remains **programmatic plus synchronized existing tools**, with a supported inspector rather than a conventional kanban/web-app workflow. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board#open-it-in-a-browser).
- **Internal origin — official self-report, medium confidence.** Vercel says it first used Board for security agents analyzing codebases, pushing fixes, and tracking work, then rebuilt v0’s “software factory” on it. Vercel claims the factory “now merges over 50% of PRs.” This is a first-party operational claim, not an independently audited adoption or quality metric. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board); [The New Stack repeats the claim with attribution](https://thenewstack.io/vercel-board-agent-swarms/).

### Inferences

- **Medium confidence:** “Harness agnostic” does **not** mean infrastructure agnostic. The agents may run elsewhere, but the canonical board, identities, grants, and stored messages are tied to a Vercel team and Vercel API. That distinction is central to an open-source alternative’s value proposition. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Medium confidence:** Board is better understood as **coordination infrastructure** than as a project-management application. Its special asset is the combination of agent-readable messages, atomic task leases, event cursors, watchers, and scoped identities—not a rich human planning UI. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Medium confidence:** The product targets teams already feeling multi-agent coordination pain, rather than solo users or teams that merely want a shared to-do list. The lead/worker, approval, and dependency workflows all presuppose several agents or an agent-plus-human loop. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#use-cases).

### Gaps

- No official customer count, active-board count, message volume, retention-based usage, paid conversion, or named external production customer was found. Internal use and the v0 claim are the only verifiable operational signals in the primary material. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- No evidence was found that “Vercel Board” previously denoted a different shipping product. This is a negative finding from launch/docs/search, not proof that no internal or temporary pre-launch name existed. [Official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available).
- The official repository confirms that the inspector reads and writes as the user and can be served from an app route. It does not establish a complete task-administration, search, or enterprise workspace feature set. [Official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board#open-it-in-a-browser).

## 2. End-to-end workflow, core entities, interaction model, integrations, and permissions

### Takeaway

A Board workflow turns a human request or agent plan into **threads that can be promoted to tasks**; workers atomically claim ready tasks under short leases, log work as replies, optionally wait for dependencies or approval, and settle with a result. What differentiates it is the closed loop between **cursor/event-driven reads, task state, scoped actor profiles, and two-way synchronization with team tools**, all mediated by Vercel credentials and channel grants. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks); [official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync).

### Cited Findings

- **Setup and first use — high confidence.** The documented path is: install the Vercel CLI and sign in; run `npx skills add vercel/board --skill board`; pass `--as <agent-name>` so the board shows the agent identity; run `vercel board whoami`; list channels; read threads; then post or claim work. On a machine, the SDK acts as the logged-in user; in a Vercel deployment, it acts as the project. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Core data model — high confidence.** The hierarchy is **Team → Board → Channel → Thread → Message**, with **Grants** and an ordered **Event log** on the board. A team has a default board named `General` and may create named boards. Channels are paths such as `dev` or `www/production` and are created by the first post. A thread is a root message plus replies. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Message and event semantics — high confidence.** Messages are Markdown and can carry labels, reactions, attachments, and schema-validated structured data. Message IDs also act as cursors, so clients can read or wait for everything after an ID. Labels are `key=value` pairs used for filtering and waiting; each board keeps one ordered change log followed by `tail`, `wait`, and watchers. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official structured-data launch section](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#share-structured-data).
- **Tasks are threads plus server-kept state — high confidence.** The root contains the instruction, replies form the work log, and Board tracks owner, claim holder, dependencies, and outcome. Any thread can become a task; task creation supports labels, assignment, attachments, prerequisites, idempotency keys, and JSON task plans. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Task lifecycle — high confidence.** Documented states are `waiting`, `ready`, `claimed`, `blocked`, `completed`, `failed`, and `canceled`. Owners or channel managers can hold, assign, reopen, or cancel tasks. A worker can `complete`, `fail`, or `release` a task; deleting a task root cancels it unless already completed. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Claiming and leases — high confidence.** `tasks claim --ready <channel>` takes the **oldest ready** matching task. By default it considers tasks assigned to the caller and unassigned tasks the caller owns; taking another owner’s tasks requires naming those owners with `--from`. When two workers race, exactly one succeeds. A lease lasts **up to five minutes** and must be renewed; after expiry, another worker can claim, and the stale worker’s later mutations fail with `claim_lost`. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Command-as-work — high confidence.** `tasks run` claims a task, runs a command with `BOARD_TASK` in its environment, renews the lease while the command runs, completes on exit code 0, fails on other exit codes, and releases on Ctrl-C or SIGTERM. This makes the CLI a worker runner, not merely a messaging client. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Dependencies and approvals — high confidence.** `--needs` creates prerequisites; a task becomes ready only after all prerequisites complete, while a failed or canceled prerequisite does not unblock dependents. `tasks block` holds work for human input or approval and ends the worker’s claim; the owner’s `unblock` returns it to `ready` or `waiting`. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Proactive execution — high confidence.** SDK watchers can run when a reply is posted or a label changes; on Vercel, events wake the deployment, avoiding a continuously running process. `vercel board schedule` lets an agent schedule a follow-up such as checking CI after five minutes. `--no-sync` keeps internal notes or check-ins off connected tools. [Official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#wake-agents-and-schedule-follow-ups).
- **Canonical human/agent example — high confidence.** A bug reported in Slack can become a Board task; an agent claims it, posts progress, opens a GitHub PR, and records the result in the same thread. For swarms, a lead decomposes a migration or security backlog into tasks with dependencies and workers execute them in parallel. [Official launch use cases](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#use-cases).
- **Actor model — high confidence.** People act as themselves; deployments act as their project; an agent profile such as `fx` is only a display/attribution name used on behalf of a person or project, shown as “fx via ada.” A profile has **no access of its own** and does not reduce the underlying credential’s permissions. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access).
- **Sync architecture — high confidence.** Sync is configured in a route in the customer’s Vercel deployment using `board({ sync: ... })`. The deployment receives platform webhooks and sends Board messages out. Vercel Connect supplies connectors so the project need not store platform secrets; Vercel’s API keeps message-to-platform mappings and wakes the route to retry failed deliveries. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync).
- **Slack integration — high confidence.** Slack is two-way: threads, messages, replies, reactions, edits, deletes, and files sync. A new Board thread can start a Slack thread and vice versa. Other apps’ Slack messages also sync; Board avoids echoing its own posts. `vercel board typing` can show an agent’s activity in the Slack thread. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#slack).
- **GitHub integration boundary — high confidence and important.** GitHub PRs and issues flow **into** Board, with comments, reviews, and inline review comments as replies. GitHub state becomes root labels (`state`, `review`, `head`, `pr`, `issue`, etc.). **Nothing goes back by default**, and a new Board thread never opens a GitHub issue or PR. Outbound replies require a custom sync function. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#github).
- **Linear integration boundary — high confidence.** Linear issues and comments flow into Board with state/priority/project/label/assignee metadata. Linear is two-way: a new thread in a mapped Board channel opens a Linear issue, using the first line as title and the whole message as description. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#linear).
- **What does not sync — high confidence.** Labels never sync out; a task’s state remains on Board; and `--no-sync` messages stay local. On a root, `--no-sync` keeps the whole thread off synced platforms even if later attached. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#what-stays-on-the-board).
- **Default access is broad — high confidence.** A new board grants every team person broad access (bounded by team role), every project’s production/preview/development deployments `read/post/react/label/sync`, and every installed team app `read/post/react/label` within app scopes. Preview deployments therefore have Board access by default. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#who-can-do-what-on-a-new-board).
- **Grant model — high confidence.** Channel grants use `read`, `post`, `react`, `label`, `sync`, and `manage`, scoped to an exact channel, subtree, or `*`. The most specific channel pattern wins, then the closest principal; tied grants add. Team roles or app scopes remain an upper bound. `manage` permits changing grants; listing all grants requires `manage` at `*`. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#permissions-and-channel-patterns).
- **Role exclusions and owner powers — high confidence.** Enterprise Viewer can read/react; Pro Viewer, Billing, Contributor, and Security roles have no Board access. Owners alone can close channels to everyone/every team person or alter the base `g1` grant; owners with `manage` may delete others’ messages and cancel others’ schedules. No one can edit another person’s message. Owners can always change grants and can grant themselves access to private channels. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#what-each-team-role-can-do); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Credential and sandbox model — high confidence.** Board issues no tokens of its own. Scripts/CI use `BOARD_TOKEN` set to a Vercel access token; deployments use OIDC; installed apps use app scopes. Code can narrow a profile with `as(profile, { can })`; `sandboxPolicy()` gives a Vercel Sandbox a network policy for the Board routes without putting the credential inside the sandbox. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#callers-without-a-vercel-login); [official launch sandbox example](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board#control-access-to-your-board).
- **Cross-platform permission leakage — high confidence and explicitly warned by Vercel.** Grants control only Board’s API. A synced channel is as readable as the Slack channel, GitHub repository, or Linear team it reaches; Board’s private grants do not protect content after it leaves. Vercel recommends matching audiences and creating private Board channels before syncing private Slack channels. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#prevent-data-spills-when-you-sync); [official overview warnings](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Agent prompt-injection/instruction risk — high confidence.** Vercel warns that agents read messages as instructions, so teams must control who can post into channels where agents work. It also tells users to keep secrets off Board because readers and synced platforms can see them. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Sync reliability limits — high confidence.** Delivery failures are retried up to five times, then recorded as `message.sync_failed` without blocking later messages. Edits, deletes, and reactions are **best effort**; a refusing platform keeps its old copy. Within a project, one deployment owns each sync key; a connector forwards webhooks to at most three destinations (production, one preview branch, one custom environment). [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#when-a-delivery-fails); [official sync deployment rules](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#which-deployment-runs-a-sync).
- **Screenshots/demo material described in text — high confidence for captions/alt text, not visual inspection.** The New Stack’s launch coverage contains three product GIFs captioned **“Slack request becomes agent tasks,” “GitHub updates appear as messages,” and “Agents request human approval.”** Its featured header image is credited to Unsplash+, not presented as a Board UI screenshot. The official repository’s `screenshot.png` alt text describes **channels, a latest-across-channels view, and a thread where people and coding agents fix a bug**. [The New Stack, 2026-11-19](https://thenewstack.io/vercel-board-agent-swarms/); [official repository screenshot](https://github.com/vercel/board#a-board-of-your-own).

### Inferences

- **Medium confidence:** The strongest interaction-model differentiator is not “chat” alone; it is **chat as a durable work queue**: cursor-based waiting, atomic leases, dependency edges, approvals, scheduled wakeups, and audit events are composed around the same thread. Generic Slack or a plain message bus would require teams to rebuild much of this. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- **Medium confidence:** Vercel deliberately leaves execution outside Board. Board coordinates and records; agents still modify code, run tests, and create PRs in their own environments. An alternative should avoid trying to become the agent runtime and instead make its protocol/storage portable across runtimes. [Official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- **Medium confidence:** The sync design is customer-deployment-operated rather than a fully opaque managed bridge. This offers customization through Chat SDK adapters but creates operational responsibility for the route, connector, and deployment. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync).
- **Medium confidence:** Attribution and authorization are intentionally separate. “fx via ada” is useful for auditability, but a malicious or over-trusted local agent still inherits ada’s access unless the caller explicitly narrows it in code. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#limit-what-an-agent-can-do).

### Gaps

- Exact webhook latency, watcher delivery guarantees, ordering guarantees across sync retries, and end-to-end task-claim consistency under partitions are not specified in the reviewed pages. Atomic single-claim behavior is documented, but broader failure semantics are not. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks); [official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync).
- No reviewed source gives an end-to-end measured setup time or comparative benchmark against Slack, Linear, GitHub Issues, or a custom queue. The demos establish intended flow, not reliability or productivity gains. [The New Stack demo coverage](https://thenewstack.io/vercel-board-agent-swarms/).
- The docs establish that agents treat messages as instructions, but the reviewed material does not describe prompt-injection defenses, content sanitization, or per-agent tool sandboxing beyond Board permissions. [Official overview warnings](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- The official repository confirms that the inspector reads and writes as the user and can be served from an app route; the remaining depth of task administration, search, and enterprise workspace features is not established by the reviewed material. [Official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board#open-it-in-a-browser).

## 3. Proprietary boundaries, export/integration and lock-in, pricing, maturity/adoption, limitations, and open-source opportunities

### Takeaway

Board’s **default hosted service is a Vercel-team experimental product, free for now on all Vercel plans**, with US-only data and no documented full-board export/import or backup/restore. However, the official `vercel/board` implementation is **Apache-2.0 open source** and supports local machines, in-memory tests, Tailscale sharing, and custom protocol-compatible backends. The open-source opportunity is therefore not to reproduce an unavailable protocol, but to provide a stronger **production self-hosted backend and control plane**—configurable residency, durable backup/export, independent identity, private defaults, and operational reliability—on top of or compatible with the published design. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board); [official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access).

### Cited Findings

- **Pricing and availability — high confidence.** Board is available as an Experimental feature on **all Vercel plans** and is **free while experimental**. Vercel explicitly says it may charge in the future. Use is subject to the Public Beta Agreement, and behavior, APIs, and limits may change. [Official overview pricing and warnings](https://vercel.com/docs/board#pricing); [official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [Public Beta Agreement](https://vercel.com/docs/release-phases/public-beta-agreement).
- **Hosted service versus open-source implementation — high confidence.** The default service runs on a Vercel team, uses Vercel credentials/API, and stores its canonical data in Vercel. Separately, Vercel publishes the `vercel/board` repository under Apache-2.0, including `@vercel/board`, the CLI, backends, inspector, skills, templates, protocol docs, and `SPEC.md`. The README says the default board “lives in Vercel’s API,” but the same handle/CLI can use a local board via `serve`, an in-memory board in tests, a Tailscale-shared local board, or a custom backend implementing the protocol. This verifies open-source client/protocol/local implementation; it does **not** establish that Vercel’s production API backend is open source. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board); [license](https://github.com/vercel/board/blob/main/LICENSE); [official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync).
- **Hosted-data boundary and residency — high confidence.** Vercel states Board stores messages, threads, tasks, labels, and attachments **in the United States**, encrypted at rest and in transit, with **no region setting**. Slack, GitHub, and Linear retain their own synced copies. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Integration surfaces and partial portability — high confidence.** The public interaction surfaces are the `vercel board` CLI, `@vercel/board` SDK, watchers, schedules, schemas, and Connect/Chat SDK adapters. The sync docs explicitly allow using a custom Chat SDK adapter instead of a connector. These are meaningful integration options, but they still target Vercel’s Board API. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board); [official sync customization](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#customize-what-syncs).
- **Extraction is not the same as export — medium-high confidence.** The CLI/SDK support reading, tailing, waiting, inspecting, and retrieving messages, and the board keeps an ordered log of changes. However, the reviewed official pages do **not** document a full-board export/import command, portable archive format, account-level backup/restore, or migration path to another provider. [Official overview data model](https://vercel.com/docs/board#data-model); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- **State that would be hard to port — high confidence for the state model, medium confidence for portability impact.** Task ownership, leases, dependencies, state, grants, profiles, schedules, watchers, structured schemas, and sync mappings live in Board/Vercel semantics. Labels and task state explicitly do not sync out, and Vercel’s API retains sync records. An external system sees message copies, not necessarily a complete reconstruction of coordination state. [Official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks); [official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#what-stays-on-the-board); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Hosted identity lock-in, with a local escape hatch — high confidence.** For the hosted Board, every caller uses a Vercel credential; Board issues no tokens of its own. People are bounded by Vercel team roles, deployments by project/OIDC, and apps by installed-app scopes. Teams avoiding Vercel identity can use the open-source local mode, including Tailscale identity for a shared local board, but the reviewed material does not present that local mode as a managed multi-tenant production service. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#callers-without-a-vercel-login); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#who-can-use-a-board); [official repository local/tailnet docs](https://github.com/vercel/board#a-board-of-your-own).
- **Agent-harness flexibility reduces one kind of lock-in — high confidence.** Any agent that can execute the CLI or call the SDK can participate; Vercel does not require its own agent runtime. This is a material advantage over a harness-bound coordinator, but it does not remove dependence on Vercel’s hosted board. [Official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- **Message/resource limits — high confidence.** A message holds up to **10,000 characters** and **10 attachments of up to 100 MB each**. Requests are rate-limited per login or deployment, and all that caller’s agents/profiles share the limit; the overview does not state the numeric rate. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Task-specific limits — high confidence.** Claim leases last up to **5 minutes**; one write creates at most **15 tasks**, or **14 with an idempotency key**; `claim --ready` searches at most **200 places** (channels × owners) and **50 channels in a subtree**; label filtering checks the **25 oldest ready tasks per place**; task changes remain in the event feed for **30 days**, while the thread and task state remain. [Official task limits](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks#task-limits).
- **Retention uncertainty — high confidence that 30 days applies only to task-change events.** The task docs explicitly distinguish the 30-day task-change event-feed window from persistent task thread/state. ### Inferences

- **Medium confidence:** Because Vercel already publishes an Apache-2.0 implementation and local/custom-backend support, the defensible open-source wedge is **not another clone of the protocol**. It is a production-grade self-hosted backend/control plane with durable storage, backup/restore and portable archives, configurable residency, independent identity, stronger private defaults, and operational tooling. Compatibility with the published protocol could reduce adoption friction. [Official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Medium confidence:** **Data residency and sovereignty** are concrete gaps for non-US or regulated teams: Board has no region setting, and synced platforms keep separate copies. A self-hosted alternative could offer configurable storage, retention, encryption keys, and audit export. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Medium confidence:** **Private-by-default and least-privilege agent identities** are a second concrete wedge. Board’s new-board defaults include all team deployments, including previews, and installed apps; profiles inherit credentials unless narrowed in application code. An alternative could make isolated agent principals, explicit approval, and per-channel defaults first-class rather than an opt-in hardening exercise. [Official access docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/access#who-can-do-what-on-a-new-board); [official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
- **Medium confidence:** **Portable export/import and vendor-neutral sync** are valuable even for Vercel users. Task state, labels, dependencies, grants, and sync mappings do not travel through the documented external copies; a documented archive/restore format and provider-neutral adapters could reduce lock-in. [Official sync docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#what-stays-on-the-board); [official tasks docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks).
- **Medium confidence:** **Human oversight without rebuilding workflow** remains an opportunity. The lightweight viewer and approval blocks are promising, but the reviewed material does not establish a complete human inbox for “decisions needed,” failed checks, missing evidence, or stalled runs. That would be a differentiated product layer over an open protocol. [Official tasks approval docs](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks#hold-a-task-for-a-person); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).
- **Medium confidence:** Early entry is possible because the category is newly launched and explicitly experimental, but “free” raises the bar: an open-source alternative cannot win on price alone. It needs to win on control, portability, trust, or workflow completeness. [Official overview pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/board#pricing); [official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available).
- **Medium confidence:** A credible alternative should preserve the best boundary Vercel draws: **coordinate agents, do not own their execution**. A narrower deep module—durable queue + event log + adapters—is more likely to fit many harnesses than a monolithic agent platform. [Official changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [official launch](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board).

### Gaps

- **Export/backup:** No official full-board export/import, backup/restore, disaster-recovery objective, or migration tooling was found in the reviewed Board documentation. A targeted `site:vercel.com/docs/board export backup retention` search returned no results. Absence in these pages is strong enough to call it undocumented, not proof that no internal facility exists. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Hosted backend source status:** The client/protocol/local implementation is verified Apache-2.0 in `vercel/board`. Whether Vercel’s production hosted API backend is open source is not established; the README distinguishes the default Vercel API board from user-run local/custom backends. [Official repository](https://github.com/vercel/board); [license](https://github.com/vercel/board/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **General retention and deletion:** No total retention period for ordinary messages/events/attachments, configurable retention, legal hold, or complete cross-platform deletion guarantee was found. The only explicit event-feed period is 30 days for task changes. [Official task limits](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/tasks#task-limits); [official sync deletion caveat](https://vercel.com/docs/board/concepts/sync#when-a-delivery-fails).
- **Reliability/SLA:** No Board-specific uptime SLA, latency objective, regional failover, or rate-limit numbers were found. Experimental beta status makes such commitments unlikely, but the reviewed material does not say so explicitly. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board).
- **Adoption/complaints:** No independent external-customer adoption metric or substantial firsthand complaint corpus was found six days after launch. The one critical X post is too thin to establish a pattern. More reporting or user research is needed before claiming widespread dissatisfaction. [Low-confidence X post](https://x.com/clairevo/status/2124908685717827855).
- **Post-launch changelog depth:** The launch changelog entry is verified. Search results also hinted at CLI release-note changes to Board identity flags, but the relevant release-note passage was not fetched or dated, so it is not treated as a confirmed post-launch change. A complete changelog audit remains open. [Official launch changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available); [CLI release notes, unverified snippet](https://vercel.com/docs/cli/release-notes).
- **Security validation:** No independent penetration-test report, SOC report specific to Board, threat model, or prompt-injection evaluation was found in the reviewed sources. Encryption and US storage are documented, but that is not equivalent to an independent security assessment. [Official overview](https://vercel.com/docs/board#what-to-know-before-you-use-board).
